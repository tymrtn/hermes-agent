# Non-commodity content strategy

## Core principle
Commodity content summarizes public knowledge. Non-commodity content packages firsthand experience, real cases, concrete evidence, and expert judgment in a way competitors cannot easily copy.

Default Hermes stance: prefer **"only we could say this"** content over generic listicles.

## Use this for
- Editorial planning
- Article ideation
- Homepage/content audits
- Membership-content positioning
- Partner/content moat discussions
- Reviewing drafts that feel too generic

## What to avoid
Do not lead with broad, interchangeable topics like:
- top 10 tips
- ultimate guides with no firsthand detail
- generic trend roundups
- lightly rewritten SEO summaries
- generic FAQ copy that reveals no judgment

These can support the library, but they should not define the brand.

## What to prefer
Prioritize content that is:
1. **Case-based** - anchored in a real person, profile, scenario, office, city, transaction, or decision
2. **Specific** - one exact problem beats a broad category
3. **Evidence-heavy** - timelines, screenshots, paperwork flows, comparisons, costs, failures, before/after, observed outcomes
4. **Judgment-rich** - explain what was chosen, rejected, or warned against, and why
5. **Hard to copy** - something a generic publisher could not credibly produce without direct experience
6. **Trust-building** - makes the audience think "they've actually seen this before"

## House formula
For every strong piece, try to include:
1. a real profile
2. one concrete problem or decision
3. the facts of the case
4. the reasoning behind the action
5. the lesson for similar readers

Template:
- **Situation:** who, where, and what they were trying to do
- **Complication:** what went wrong or what tradeoff appeared
- **Evidence:** documents, timeline, quotes, costs, screenshots, or observations
- **Judgment:** why this path was chosen or rejected
- **Takeaway:** what a similar reader should do differently

## Good title patterns
- Why this [application / purchase / move / filing] failed after [specific event]
- What changed between [city A] and [city B] in the same process
- We thought [assumption] was fine - until [specific consequence]
- The [amount] mistake that came from misunderstanding [rule]
- Why I told this client not to [popular/default choice]
- We compared [number] real [documents/listings/options] - here is what the fine print actually meant

## Review test
A piece is probably still commodity if a competitor could swap in their brand name and publish nearly the same article.

A piece is probably non-commodity if it contains:
- real operational detail
- distinctive reasoning
- local variation
- proof of experience
- an opinion that costs something to hold

## SpainExpat-style examples
- NLV delayed because of one missing bank letter
- TIE outcomes compared across provinces
- padrón rejected because the rental setup looked valid but failed in practice
- accidental Spanish tax residency with real consequences
- rental or bank fine-print teardown with real examples

## Strategic role
Use non-commodity content to:
- build trust fast
- differentiate from SEO farms and generic AI content
- support premium offers and memberships
- make partners and readers feel the brand has lived experience
- create a content moat that is not easily copied

## Practical rule for Hermes agents
When proposing or drafting content, first ask:
- What is the real case here?
- What do we know from experience, evidence, or observed outcomes?
- What judgment can we show, not just describe?
- Why would this be hard for a generic writer to fake?

If those answers are weak, tighten the topic before drafting.
